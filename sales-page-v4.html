01 — Templates

A library, not a prompt

Lower thirds, maps, tables, charts. Built once as functions, then reskinned to your brand. Your designer owns the look; the logic is already proven.

02 — Data

The number changes, the graphic changes

Nothing is baked in. A pre-rendered file is wrong the moment the score moves. Pellar renders vectors in real time, so a correction costs a sentence.

03 — Control

Hands stay where they are

Voice, text, or the panel. Whichever is fastest at the moment you need it — without leaving the desk you are already working at.

Why it matters

Three minutes is an eternity

Changing one digit on a pre-rendered graphic means opening After Effects, editing a text layer, re-exporting, re-importing and re-triggering. Three to five minutes, while you are on air. That is the problem Pellar exists to remove.

Before it reaches air

Every template
passes verify

A template is checked before it can be deployed — formatting, overflow, whether it conforms to the standard the rest of your library is built on. Nothing reaches the playout chain unchecked.

That check is also a record. As AI-assisted content moves inside the scope of European broadcast rules, being able to show what was verified, and when, stops being a nicety.

Where it sits

It fits the gallery
you already run

A graphic operator builds it. An editor checks it. A vision mixer takes it. Pellar does not ask you to collapse those roles — it gives each of them the permissions that match what they already do.

Operator

Builds and cues

Drives the panel by voice or text. Sees the preview before anyone else does.

Editor

Checks and approves

Reviews what is queued against what is going out. Approval is a role, not a habit.

Designer

Owns the library

Curates templates, guarantees the pixels, decides what is allowed to be published to the live system at all.

Access

Tier and role decide what can reach air

Who can build, who can publish to the live system, and who can only preview — set per account, not per good intention.

Output

Into the stack you own

Browser source or NDI, into the switcher or the stream you are already running.

The fast path

Voice, when your
hands are full

Say the change and it happens. It is not the whole product — the template library is — but it is the difference between reacting to a goal in two seconds and reacting to it in two minutes.

Also drivable by text, or straight from the panel.

Built to run on your hardware

No outside
dependency on air

Pellar is being built so voice recognition and rendering can run on your own machines — because a broadcast cannot be hostage to somebody else’s API being up, and some newsrooms cannot send a single frame outside the building.

Local model support is in testing. It is an architectural commitment, not a shipped feature — we will say so plainly when it lands.
